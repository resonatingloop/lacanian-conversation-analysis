# Source registry v0.1

This is a compact warrant registry, not a claim that Lacanian discourse analysis has one settled method. Repository-authored instructions are paraphrases and operational syntheses. No third-party source text is bundled.

## Sources

### `LACAN-ECRITS`

Jacques Lacan. *Écrits: The First Complete Edition in English*. Translated by Bruce Fink. New York: W. W. Norton, 2006.

- Role: primary theoretical lineage for signifier, subject, address, speech, and knowledge.
- Access: publisher description at <https://wwnorton.co.uk/books/9780393329254-ecrits>.
- Reuse: commercial/citation-only for this repository. Do not copy or bundle the book.

### `PARKER-2005`

Ian Parker. “Lacanian Discourse Analysis in Psychology: Seven Theoretical Elements.” *Theory & Psychology* 15, no. 2 (2005): 163–182. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0959354305051361>.

- Role: explicit discourse-analytic bridge among formal text, anchoring representation, agency and determination, knowledge, positions in language, deadlocks, and interpretation.
- Access: publisher abstract and restricted article access.
- Reuse: citation-only unless a separate license is obtained.

### `SWALES-2026`

Stephanie Swales. “Lacanian Discourse Analysis.” In *The Routledge International Handbook of Psychoanalysis, Subjectivity, and Technology*. Routledge, 2026. <https://doras.dcu.ie/32864/>.

- Role: contemporary account of Lacanian discourse analysis as grounded in Lacanian theory while resisting a fixed coding grid.
- Access: open repository manuscript.
- Reuse: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 as stated by the repository record. Attribute any reused passage; this project currently paraphrases and links only.

### `PAVON-CUELLAR-2017`

David Pavón-Cuéllar. “Marxism, Psychoanalysis and Lacanian Discourse Analysis.” In *Marxism and Psychoanalysis: In or Against Psychology?* Routledge, 2017. Public manuscript: <https://discourseunit.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/arcpdavidp.pdf>.

- Role: historical and immanent account of why Lacanian discourse analysis should not become a decontextualized technical grid.
- Access: public manuscript.
- Reuse: no explicit reuse license established in this project; citation-only.

### `FRONTIERS-2020`

Christian Dunker and Ian Parker, eds. “Lacanian Psychoanalysis: Revolutions in Subjectivity.” *Frontiers in Psychology* (2020). <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00192/full>.

- Role: openly accessible contemporary orientation to Lacanian work across discourse, subjectivity, and social relations.
- Access: open access.
- Reuse: Creative Commons Attribution under the Frontiers article license.

### `CA-SYSTEMATICS-1974`

Harvey Sacks, Emanuel A. Schegloff, and Gail Jefferson. “A Simplest Systematics for the Organization of Turn-Taking for Conversation.” *Language* 50, no. 4 (1974): 696–735. <https://doi.org/10.2307/412243>.

- Role: sequential grounding for turns, transition relevance, and locally managed organization.
- Access: bibliographic record and public-access copy linked by the Max Planck Institute at <https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item2376846/simplest-systematics-organization-turn-taking-conversation>.
- Reuse: no redistribution license established here; citation-only.

### `CA-REPAIR-1977`

Emanuel A. Schegloff, Gail Jefferson, and Harvey Sacks. “The Preference for Self-Correction in the Organization of Repair in Conversation.” *Language* 53, no. 2 (1977): 361–382. <https://doi.org/10.2307/413107>.

- Role: sequential grounding for trouble sources, repair initiation, and repair trajectories.
- Access: bibliographic access through the DOI and research-library records.
- Reuse: citation-only unless a source-specific license is established.

## Public primary-text lead not bundled

The École lacanienne de psychanalyse hosts “Pas-tout Lacan,” a public web archive of more than 400 Lacan texts and interventions: <https://ecole-lacanienne.net/bibliolacan/pas-tout-lacan/>. It is useful for source discovery, but this project found no blanket reuse license on the collection page. Treat it as read/link/cite material, not as a corpus to vendor.

## Source policy

- Prefer primary texts for theoretical claims and conversation-analysis originals for sequence claims.
- Use open scholarship to expose the interpretive bridge and its disagreements.
- Record access and reuse separately: public-to-read is not open-source.
- Do not make the user reconstruct a hidden bibliography. Name the source key next to any contested or extended concept explanation.
- Do not quote a source from memory. Verify the passage against an authorized copy when exact wording matters.
