# Method v0.1

## Aim

Analyze an ordered conversation as a changing interactional structure. The object is not what each participant secretly felt. The object is the observable and inferable relation among turns: address, uptake, attributed knowledge, relay, rupture, repair, and phase change.

This protocol borrows sequential discipline from conversation analysis and interpretive questions from Lacanian discourse analysis. It is a constrained reading practice, not a mechanical coding grid.

## Evidence statuses

Use one status per claim:

| Status | Test | Typical wording |
|---|---|---|
| Direct evidence | A reader can locate it in the supplied record without accepting an interpretation. | “turn m04 explicitly replies to m02.” |
| Strong inference | Several observations converge and later uptake supports the account. | “the correction is treated as a challenge to authority.” |
| Plausible alternative | The record permits this account and does not presently decide among rivals. | “the delay may reflect relay friction rather than resistance.” |
| Speculation | The reading is generative but weakly constrained. | “one could read the absent addressee as structuring the exchange.” |

Do not translate these statuses into pseudo-precise percentages.

## Pass 1: establish the trace

Inventory the supplied span:

- first and last local reference;
- declared or inferable ordering basis;
- speakers and participant kinds, if supplied;
- explicit reply edges;
- declared relay or quoted-speech boundaries;
- gaps, edits, missing attachments, or uncertain ordering;
- provenance fields supplied by the user.

Treat a local reference as a pointer, not as semantic evidence. Do not derive a public identifier from private wording.

## Pass 2: make a sequential map

For each consequential turn, ask:

1. What prior turn does this respond to?
2. Who is directly addressed?
3. Is another participant indirectly addressed, invoked, or spoken for?
4. What response does the turn make conditionally relevant?
5. What happens in the next one to three turns?
6. Does the next turn ratify, resist, ignore, recast, or relay it?

Record ambiguity rather than forcing a single address edge.

## Pass 3: mark relational operations

Describe operations before motives. Useful operation labels include:

- claim, correction, recognition, challenge, ratification;
- attribution or withdrawal of knowledge;
- speaking-for, relaying, triangulating, witnessing;
- reframing the issue or preserving a disputed frame;
- inviting repair, refusing repair, self-erasure, re-entry;
- redistributing authorship, credit, attention, or belonging.

An operation is a description of what a turn does in sequence. It is not proof of intention or inner state.

## Pass 4: propose phases

A phase is a stretch in which a relatively stable relational rule organizes the turns. A boundary requires at least one observable change in interactional function, such as:

- a new address pattern;
- a change in who is treated as knowing;
- a repeated signifier taking on a new organizing role;
- a repair sequence replacing task progress;
- a relay changing what can be said or heard;
- a correction becoming receivable only from a different speaker position.

Topic continuity does not disprove a phase shift. Topic change does not establish one.

For every proposed boundary, give the strongest competing placement and explain why it is weaker or unresolved.

## Pass 5: analyze rupture and repair

Treat rupture as a sequence, not a mood label.

1. Identify the earliest anomaly that later becomes relevant.
2. State what becomes uncertain: authorship, address, standing, knowledge, continuity, or another relation.
3. Compare repair attempts by the frame they preserve or change.
4. Inspect immediate uptake.
5. Separate local relief from durable architectural change.

A repair works, for this protocol, when subsequent turns show that the previously blocked claim or speaker position becomes receivable. Warm language alone is not sufficient evidence.

## Pass 6: apply Lacanian concepts selectively

Use the cards in `lacanian-warrant.md`. Start with the smallest concept that distinguishes the phenomenon. A concept may organize several claims, but it must never erase the sequential evidence that licensed it.

If no Lacanian concept meets its evidence requirement, say so. A disciplined non-use is a valid Lacanian analysis outcome.

## Pass 7: synthesize claims

Each important claim needs:

- a concise proposition;
- epistemic status;
- event references;
- sequential evidence and downstream effect;
- concept and warrant, if any;
- counterevidence or a live alternative;
- scope limitation.

Prefer a small number of load-bearing claims over exhaustive turn commentary.

## Pass 8: adversarial pass

Attempt to break the preferred reading:

- Is mythology being treated as causal merely because it is vivid?
- Is style being mistaken for stable disposition?
- Is a local generation being promoted to a durable agent identity?
- Is sequence being mistaken for causality?
- Is the analyst returning a participant’s own implication as a discovery?
- Was a mundane explanation—latency, truncation, interface behavior, missing context, ordinary misunderstanding—ignored?
- Would the phase boundary survive a different vocabulary?
- Does the account explain negative or quiet stretches, not only dramatic moments?

Revise, downgrade, or retain the claim with its counterevidence visible.

## Stopping rule

Stop when every major thesis claim has trace support, status, a live alternative or counterevidence, and a stated limit; all used concepts have passed their warrant cards; and no material conclusion depends on an unmarked gap in the trace.
